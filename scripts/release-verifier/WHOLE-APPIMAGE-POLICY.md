# DRAFT — independent whole-AppImage approval policy

**Status: experimental draft; not production-enabled, root-approved, or authorization to publish.** `src/opaque.rs` contains an experimental implementation of the fixed encoding below; it is not an approved production wire format or a complete policy implementation. No automatic approval or publication is authorized. This proposes opaque whole-file byte hashing as an alternative to parsing AppRun for this particular byte-identity decision; it does not establish that the image is safe, correctly built, or executable. Adopting it requires a separately approved policy change and independent security review; it does not silently waive the current AppRun-reference or isolation requirements.

The actual `src/lib.rs` production entry points reject unconditionally. Its test-only manifest uses domain `XJAUS-RELEASE-MANIFEST-v1\0`, version `1.2.3`, a fixed 40-character commit and five ordered artifact records; it does not implement this approval. Its test-only callback and experimental AppRun reference are not production controls. See `README.md` and `CONTROLLED-CONSUMER-DESIGN.md` for existing limitations.

## Roles and trust

Two distinct human principals hold separate responsibilities and separately authenticated Ed25519 keys: the **manifest issuer** signs the release manifest; the **independent approver** signs the whole-AppImage approval. Neither principal may act for the other on the same release, approve their own work, share signing credentials, or delegate to an agent acting as their proxy for self-approval. Different key bytes alone do not prove independent people or independent review. Organization policy must establish independence, separation of duties and key custody, including resistance to common control and conflicts of interest; if that cannot be established, reject.

A separately authorized trust policy must bind each role to its named principal and public-key fingerprint, independently authenticate the anchors and their role assignments out of band, require different principals and different public keys, and define key rotation, revocation and incident response. The candidate, manifest, approval, caller and signing service cannot supply or override their own trust anchors or role assignments. Test keys are never production keys. A compromised principal, key, trust policy or host defeats the corresponding guarantee; signatures authenticate possession of a trusted key, not the actual quality or independence of review.

## Human prerequisites before approval

Before signing, the independent approver personally reviews authenticated evidence from sources independent of the manifest issuer and candidate-provided assertions: the intended release version and full source commit; source/build provenance, clean or explicitly accounted-for tree, build inputs and pinned toolchain, build procedure and logs; and the origin and custody of the exact image. They independently obtain and verify the exact filename, byte length and whole-file SHA-256 over every byte from offset zero through EOF, using trusted tools on the actual candidate or an independently authenticated identical copy. They compare those values with the intended manifest record and their own evidence, resolve discrepancies, and record the evidence and review decision through an authenticated auditable process. A hash computed solely from the candidate or a manifest issuer's statement proves neither independent provenance nor safety. If evidence or independence is insufficient, do not sign. A material rebuild or change to any bound field requires fresh review and a new approval.

## Experimental implemented approval encoding v1

The following describes the exact bytes constructed by `src/opaque.rs`, not a generalized proposed format. All integers are unsigned big-endian. The detached signature is exactly 64 bytes, ordinary Ed25519 over **all** bytes of the canonical message below, including the domain; the public key is the independently provisioned 32-byte approver key, never a key embedded in the message. The distinct domain prevents interpreting this signature as the existing manifest signature; keys and issuers must also be distinct.

| Order | Field | Canonical encoding |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Domain and schema version | Exact ASCII `XJAUS-OPAQUE-APPIMAGE-APPROVAL-v1` followed by one NUL byte; the `v1` is the schema version. |
| 2 | Release version | Exactly the 5 ASCII bytes `1.2.3`, with no length prefix. |
| 3 | Full commit | Exactly the 40 ASCII bytes `80529c750da5a6bf684391333d5f4e49c574fbee`, with no length prefix. |
| 4 | Artifact name | u16 byte length followed by the fixed ASCII path `linux/XJAUS-1.2.3-x86_64.AppImage`. |
| 5 | Byte size | u64, supplied to the approval encoder; the experimental manifest verifier separately requires positive sizes and applies per-file and aggregate limits. |
| 6 | Whole-file SHA-256 | Exactly 32 raw bytes; SHA-256 of the complete sealed AppImage bytes, exactly equal to the unique manifest record and independent approval evidence. |

The approval verifier reconstructs these exact bytes from the size and digest and compares the entire supplied approval before verifying the signature; it rejects any differing or trailing bytes. It checks 32-byte keys, unequal key bytes, and a 64-byte signature. Those checks do not establish distinct principals, independent review, trusted anchors, revocation, freshness, or global conflict detection. The following are additional proposed policy requirements, not all implemented: enforce bounded message lengths and checked arithmetic before allocation, reject truncation, unsupported domains/versions and malformed lengths; authenticate the exact received bytes and then parse those same bytes, rejecting anything noncanonical. Never parse, normalize and reserialize a different message for signature verification. The independently authenticated manifest must separately bind the same exact tuple. Require exactly one AppImage record and one applicable approval for that tuple; reject duplicate records, duplicate approval objects, conflicting approvals, or ambiguity rather than selecting the first or last. Other release artifacts remain subject to their own manifest checks and policy.

## Proposed verification and consumption boundary

1. Establish a trusted host, kernel, verifier, dependencies, role policy and independently authenticated keys. Reject absent, revoked, ambiguous or unauthorized role assignments, identical keys or principals, bad signatures and every policy mismatch. Authenticate both the existing manifest under the manifest issuer's key and the separate approval under the approver's key with their distinct domains. Do not accept caller-selected keys or policy.
2. Check the exact authorized version, full commit and complete expected artifact set against the authenticated manifest; check the approval tuple against its single matching AppImage record and independently authorized release identity. Check all other manifest-bound artifacts under their applicable rules. A valid approval does not replace manifest authentication, independent human review or provenance.
3. Under trusted, quiescent staging throughout enumeration and acquisition, open fixed paths descriptor-relatively without following symlinks, reject nonregular files, extra/missing entries, unsafe paths and errors. Apply bounded resource limits and copy candidate bytes into sealed snapshots; verify all required seals, exact lengths and SHA-256 over the complete sealed bytes. Compare the AppImage snapshot to **both** authenticated records. Verify every required artifact before any consumer runs. Handle shared descriptor offsets explicitly or use offset-independent reads. The experimental implementation has size limits but **no whole-pipeline timeout**; acquisition and hashing still need explicit memory, I/O and wall-time controls, with no claim of protection from uninterruptible I/O.
4. Discard staging paths and descriptors before the boundary. Only a fixed, reviewed, in-tree, non-publishing consumer receives the authenticated metadata and the same sealed descriptors; no path reopen, caller callback, plugin or user-selected executable, candidate execution, publication, signing, network delivery or other external side effect. On any failure, close resources and return no success report. The consumer contract is trusted-code discipline, **not an OS sandbox**; hostile in-process code can perform syscalls. Stronger isolation requires a separately designed and verified OS boundary.

Staging enumeration, descriptor checks and sequential copies are **not an atomic snapshot**. They cannot prove quiescence or prevent transient substitutions, concurrent in-place writes, or a malicious same-UID process. The trusted staging assumption must cover the entire acquisition and validation interval; ordinary permissions alone do not establish it. Sealing protects the resulting snapshot after sealing, not provenance or the source's historical state. This design does not defend against a compromised host or kernel.

## Replay, rollback and equivocation — unimplemented external controls

A signature has no inherent freshness, expiry, revocation knowledge or one-time-use property. This schema has no timestamp, nonce, sequence number or unique approval ID. Within one verification reject duplicate or conflicting records and approvals; across runs an identical valid approval can be replayed. Exact version/commit/path/size/digest binding prevents its use for a *different* tuple, not reuse for the same tuple. An attacker able to substitute an old but valid authorized policy and both old signatures can cause rollback unless a separately authenticated, rollback-resistant policy or state pins the currently authorized release. Enforce that state before acceptance; without it, explicitly claim no rollback protection.

The tuple alone cannot prevent an authorized issuer or approver from signing two different digests for the same release identity, nor detect conflicting approvals withheld from this verifier. Detection or prevention requires independent, authenticated, durable, rollback-resistant release/approval state with a defined uniqueness rule, or an independently witnessed append-only transparency mechanism and consistency checks. Without such an external mechanism, reject conflicts that are actually presented but claim no global equivocation detection or prevention. Key revocation and compromise handling likewise require current authenticated external trust state; offline verification cannot learn later revocations.

## Conditions for future implementation

Before any enablement, separately approve the schema and role policy; provision and independently verify trust anchors; establish evidence retention, role independence and rollback/equivocation controls; and obtain separate authorization and independent security review. Add adversarial tests for signed malformed and duplicate inputs, cross-domain/key/role confusion, each tuple mismatch, replay and conflicting approvals, stale or revoked trust state, path substitution, missing seals, snapshot mutation and post-boundary path access. Demonstrate that all failed checks prevent consumer invocation and effects, and that the consumer reads the very snapshots hashed. Verify resource controls over the whole acquisition pipeline. Passing these tests is not itself approval, publication authorization, or proof against a compromised host. Until then production entry points remain disabled and no release or publication is authorized.
